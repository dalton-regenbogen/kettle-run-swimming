Swim meets 101

How high school meets work, for swimmers and families

How our meets work

Most of our regular-season meets bring several teams to one pool. Even so, they’re scored as a set of dual meets run at the same time: Kettle Run is scored against each other team separately, so one meet can count as two, three, or more wins and losses. That’s why the Results page shows a score against each opponent. Boys and girls are scored separately too.

Order of events

The standard high school order. Diving is only at meets that include it.

  1. 200 Medley Relay
  2. 200 Freestyle
  3. 200 Individual Medley
  4. 50 Freestyle
  5. Diving
  6. 100 Butterfly
  7. 100 Freestyle
  8. 500 Freestyle
  9. 200 Freestyle Relay
  10. 100 Backstroke
  11. 100 Breaststroke
  12. 400 Freestyle Relay

Entries

High school rules let each swimmer swim up to four events at a meet, with no more than two individual events. How many swimmers each team may enter in an event depends on the meet: some meets allow more entries per event, so more of our swimmers get to race, and some allow fewer. The coach sets the lineup for each meet with that in mind.

The postseason

After the regular season come three championship meets: Districts, then Regionals, then States. Swimmers move on by finishing high enough at the meet before, and swimmers can also earn a spot at States with a state qualifying time.

At the meet

Swimmers

  • Arrive at the pool (or school, if there’s a bus) by the time the coach sets.
  • Bring your suit, goggles, team caps, team shirt and jacket, a towel, and a water bottle.
  • Stay with the team and cheer for your teammates.

Families

  • Check the meet schedule for the date, start time, and pool.
  • Pool decks are warm and busy—spectators usually sit in the stands.
  • We need volunteers at every meet. See Volunteering.

Volunteering & officiating

Our meets only run with parent volunteers

Every meet needs a set number of volunteers from each team, and we rely on our families to fill those spots. No experience is needed for most roles—you’ll be shown what to do on the day. Your help keeps meets safe, organized, and positive for every swimmer, and we’re grateful for every parent who steps up.

Volunteer roles

Timer
Times one lane from the end of the pool. The most common role, and the easiest to start with.
Head timer
Runs the timers, keeps backup times, and steps in if a watch fails.
Runner
Collects times and results from the deck and brings them to the scoring table.
Deck marshal
Helps keep the pool deck safe and orderly during warm-ups and the meet.
Computer
Runs the meet software for entries and results. Good for anyone comfortable with a laptop.
Official
Makes sure races follow the rules. Takes certification—see below.

To sign up, watch for the volunteer sign-up from our parent volunteer coordinator, or email the coach.

Become an official

We need more stroke & turn officials

Becoming an official is one of the most helpful ways to support the team—and every meet needs them.

Stroke & turn officials watch swimmers’ strokes, turns, and finishes to make sure every race is fair. Officials are trained and certified before working meets. If you’re interested, or just curious what’s involved, reach out to the coach.

Team policies

Attendance, lettering, and expectations from the team handbook

Attendance

Expectations depend on which kind of athlete you are—see two ways to be on the team. Either way, tell the coach about any absence before it happens—ideally the swimmer, but a parent is fine—through the team group chat, email, a call, or a text. Every absence is recorded.

What counts as excused

A sick day from school, or a scheduled school event that conflicts with practice. Other situations are reviewed case by case—but they still need to be communicated as soon as possible.

Earning a varsity letter

To letter, a swimmer can have no more than:

3

Unexcused practice absences

Over the whole season

1

Unexcused meet absence

Over the whole season

Every day, whether or not you plan to letter

On time and prepared
For practice: suit, goggles, cap, swim gear, and a refillable water bottle, ready at the start time. For meets: at the pool (or school, for the bus) with all your gear by the arrival time. Tardiness is recorded.
Positive attitude and sportsmanship
Before, during, and after every practice and meet: no smack talk toward teammates or opponents, no foul language, nothing that brings others down. Be a team player. Issues are handled with a verbal warning first, then a parent contact, then a KRHS administrator.
Follow school and FCPS rules
Nothing that breaks the law, the FCPS Code of Student Conduct, or the FCPS Athletic Handbook—including alcohol, drugs, or inhalants. A suspension from KRHS during the season means dismissal from the team and no letter.
Be safe
At the pool, in public areas, and in the parking lot—it’s busy and not a place for horseplay. Student drivers must follow Virginia’s passenger laws and drive carefully in the WARF lot. Once at the pool, swimmers can’t leave without the coach’s permission.

Apparel & gear

What the team provides, and what’s optional

Provided by the team
Two KRHS swim caps, a team t-shirt (covered by the t-shirt fee), and a team jacket for the season—handed out at the first meet.
Team suit (optional)
Not required. Any plain suit works for meets—black is preferred—with no logos from other teams or programs. A durable, competition-style suit is best.
Spirit wear (optional)

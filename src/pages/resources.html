Getting there

Practices, away meets, and home meets

Practices
No school transportation. Families arrange rides to and from WARF, and student drivers must follow Virginia’s passenger laws. Bring a suit, goggles, a cap, and a water bottle. Practice schedule →
Away meets (any meet not at WARF)
Swimmers ride the team bus to the meet. Afterward they can go home with a parent, or with another family, but a parent or guardian must sign them out (and note who they’re leaving with).
Meets at WARF
There’s no bus to home meets, so swimmers need their own ride to and from WARF, just like practice. No sign-out needed.

Attendance & lettering

Reporting an absence, and earning a varsity letter

Reporting an absence

Swimmers tell the coach before the practice or meet. Every absence is recorded.

  • Team chat

    Post in the team GroupMe chat.

  • GroupMe message

    Message the coach in a GroupMe message that includes a parent.

  • Email

    Email the coach with a parent copied.

Swimmers can’t call or text the coach, or message one-on-one. Another adult is always included.

What counts as excused

A sick day from school, or a scheduled school event that conflicts with practice. Other situations are reviewed case by case, but they still need to be communicated as soon as possible.

Swim meets 101

How high school meets work

How our meets are scored

Most regular-season meets bring several teams to one pool, but they’re scored as a set of dual meets run at the same time: Kettle Run is scored against each other team separately, so one meet can count as two, three, or more wins and losses. Boys and girls are scored separately too. That’s why Results lists a score against each opponent.

Order of events

The standard high school order. Diving is only at meets that include it.

  1. 200 Medley Relay
  2. 200 Freestyle
  3. 200 Individual Medley
  4. 50 Freestyle
  5. Diving
  6. 100 Butterfly
  7. 100 Freestyle
  8. 500 Freestyle
  9. 200 Freestyle Relay
  10. 100 Backstroke
  11. 100 Breaststroke
  12. 400 Freestyle Relay

Entries

Each swimmer can swim up to four events at a meet, with no more than two individual events. How many swimmers each team may enter in an event depends on the meet: some allow more entries, so more of our swimmers race, and some allow fewer. The coach sets each meet’s lineup with that in mind.

The postseason

Three championship meets after the regular season, in this order

  1. 1. Districts

    The Northwestern District championship. No qualifying times: the only limit is the meet’s entry limits.

  2. 2. Regionals

    The Class 3 Region B championship. Also no qualifying times, only entry limits.

  3. 3. States

    Swimmers qualify by place at Regionals (top 5 in individual events, top 4 in relays) or by swimming a state qualifying time.

At the meet

Swimmers

Arrive at the pool (or school, if there’s a bus) by the time the coach sets.

Bring your suit, goggles, team caps, team shirt and jacket, a towel, and a water bottle.

Stay with the team and cheer for your teammates.

Families

Families are encouraged to come to meets and cheer on the team.

Check the meet schedule for the date, start time, and pool.

Every meet runs on parent volunteers, so please plan to help at a few. See Volunteering.

Volunteering & officiating

Our meets only run with parent volunteers

Every meet needs a set number of volunteers from each team, and we rely on our families to fill those spots. No experience is needed for most roles: you’ll be shown what to do on the day.

Volunteer roles

Timer
Times one lane from the end of the pool. The most common role, and the easiest to start with.
Head timer
Runs the timers, keeps backup times, and steps in if a watch fails.
Runner
Collects times and results from the deck and brings them to the scoring table.
Deck marshal
Helps keep the pool deck safe and orderly during warm-ups and the meet.
Computer
Runs the meet software for entries and results.
Official
Makes sure races follow the rules. Takes certification (see below).

To sign up, watch for the volunteer sign-up from our parent volunteer coordinator, or email the coach.

Become an official

We need more stroke & turn officials

It’s one of the most helpful ways to support the team, and every meet needs them.

Stroke & turn officials watch swimmers’ strokes, turns, and finishes to make sure every race is fair. Officials are trained and certified before working meets. Interested, or just curious? Reach out.

Apparel & gear

What the team provides, and what’s optional

Swim caps
Provided by the team.
Team jacket
Signed out for the season. Return it at the end, or buy it to keep.
Team T-shirt
Paid for by the T-shirt fee on student accounts, and handed out at the first meet.
Team suit (optional)
Any plain suit works for meets (black is preferred), with no logos from other teams or programs. A durable, competition-style suit is best.
Spirit wear (optional)
KRHS swim gear for swimmers and families who want to show Cougar pride. Not required, and it has no effect on being on the team. Team apparel store ↗
